Growth & Resilience Check-In
Working draft

Use these as a starting point and adjust any details so they reflect your own
experience and voice.

1. Growth & Reflection

Think of a recent situation when you felt you were at your best in your role.
What were you doing, and what contributed to that experience?

I feel at my best when I can take a complicated, ambiguous problem and turn it
into a careful, usable path forward. A recent example has been my SharePoint
migration work: tracing document-link and permission issues, building tools to
identify and review changes, and making sure proposed fixes can be checked
before anything is applied. I focus on details that matter in practice, like
preserving existing metadata, making incomplete coverage visible, and verifying
results rather than assuming a successful run means the work is done.

That experience brought together problem-solving, ownership, and a clear
connection to helping colleagues find and use the right information. I was
particularly effective when I could work through the evidence methodically,
keep the risks visible, and deliver something repeatable instead of a one-off
fix.

Looking back at your goals this year, what would you approach differently
that you can apply going forward?

I would make progress and decision points more visible earlier, especially on
work that has many dependencies or edge cases. I can get deeply focused on
making a solution reliable, which is valuable, but I can do a better job of
sharing a small working version, open questions, and tradeoffs before all the
details are settled. Going forward, I want to break large efforts into clearer
milestones, ask for review at those points, and reserve time for documentation
and handoff alongside implementation.

2. Resilience & Belonging

What has helped you stay motivated, adaptable, or resilient when facing
change, setbacks, or challenges?

I stay motivated by connecting the technical work to its practical impact:
reducing friction for colleagues and helping important information remain
accessible and trustworthy. When an issue is more complicated than it first
appears, I try to slow down, gather evidence, separate what is known from what
still needs validation, and work through the problem in manageable steps.
Preview-first workflows, explicit safeguards, and verification help me adapt
without taking unnecessary risks. I also try to treat setbacks as useful
information about what the process or tool needs to handle better next time.

What has your leader and/or team done that has helped you feel supported,
included, and connected to your work? What else would strengthen your sense
of belonging?

I feel most supported when the team shares the context behind a request, is
open to questions, and gives me room to take ownership while staying available
for feedback and decisions. Knowing how the work affects colleagues helps me
prioritize and stay connected to its purpose. I would be strengthened by
continuing to have regular opportunities to share progress and lessons learned,
get timely feedback when priorities change, and hear from the people who use
the tools and processes I work on.

3. Team Cohesion

What helps you feel connected and engaged with your team, particularly during
busy or challenging periods?

Clear priorities, candid communication, and a shared understanding of what
needs attention help me stay engaged when things are busy. It is also helpful
when people can raise risks or uncertainty early without blame, and when we
make time to document decisions so others can pick up the work. Small check-ins
and practical feedback keep work from becoming isolated and help the team adjust
together.

Share a recent example of how you contributed to your team’s success or helped
create a positive experience for a colleague.

In the SharePoint migration work, I have contributed by turning complex
remediation tasks into reviewable steps and tools rather than asking colleagues
to rely on opaque, one-shot changes. For example, the System Editor cleanup was
structured around a read-only preview, explicit scope and error reporting, and
a separate guarded apply phase. The reviewed preview documented 14,623
candidates with zero recorded issues, giving the team evidence to make an
informed next-step decision. More broadly, clear reports, dry-run options, and
verification checks make it easier for others to understand what a script will
do, spot problems before changes are made, and trust the outcome.
